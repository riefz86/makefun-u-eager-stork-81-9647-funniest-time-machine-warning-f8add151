# Notes — Funniest Time Machine Warning Label

## Final entry (106 characters)

> WARNING: Never step out before 1900. Your great-grandfather is armed — and he has already read this label.

Why it was chosen: it turns a real time-travel hazard (meeting — and
endangering — an ancestor) into the joke instead of naming it flatly; the
surprise is saved for the last clause; it is phrased like a genuine safety
notice, so the joke cuts against official calm; and the punchline is a
causal loop — the warning label itself is what put the traveller in
danger. No word is wasted inside the 120-character limit.

## Discarded alternatives

1. "WARNING: Do not meet yourself. He already knows what you did, and he
   has filed a complaint." — the duplicate-self angle is the most obvious
   time-travel joke in the field; the punchline lands mid-sentence, so
   nothing is saved for the end.
2. "WARNING: Never travel to the day you were born. The delivery room is
   small, and your mother has questions." — the hazard is only implied,
   "has questions" is a soft closer, and it reads more as a joke about
   birth than as a warning label.
3. "CAUTION: This machine returns you to the exact moment you left. Your
   past self is already inside it, and he is not sharing." — a decent
   paradox, but the setup is long and "he is not sharing" is a weak final
   beat.
4. "WARNING: Do not kill your grandfather. He is the only one who knows
   where the spare key is." — the grandfather paradox is the single most
   overused time-travel joke; it fails the judge's freshness criterion
   outright.
5. "WARNING: If you see yourself, do not wave. He is you, and he is
   already disappointed." — flat closer; "disappointed" is a generic
   AI-joke ending with no paradox payoff.
